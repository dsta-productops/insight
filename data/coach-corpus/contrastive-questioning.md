# Contrastive Questioning

## Principle

Asking how something was done *before* an alternative existed — or how it is done when the tool is unavailable — surfaces real baseline behaviour, priorities, and workarounds. Participants struggle to articulate the value of a tool in the abstract, but they can describe concretely what life was like without it. The contrast exposes what the tool actually changed, which is often different from what it was designed to change.

## Method

1. Identify the tool, feature, or process under discussion.
2. Ask for the before-state: "How did you handle this before X existed?" / "What did you do last time X was down?"
3. Probe the delta: "What changed when you switched?" "What do you miss from the old way?"
4. Listen for retained workarounds — parts of the old process that survived indicate needs the new tool doesn't meet.

## Variants

- **Tool-removal contrast**: "If X disappeared tomorrow, what would you do first?"
- **Cross-context contrast**: "You do this at work with X — how do you do the same thing at home?"
- **Peer contrast**: "How do colleagues who don't use X handle this?"

## Detection patterns (when a coach should suggest this)

- Participant describes current tool usage but the baseline has never been established.
- Participant makes a comparative claim ("it's so much better now") without the 'before' being described concretely.
- Discussion of adoption or switching where the previous workflow hasn't been asked about.
- Participant mentions old habits or legacy systems in passing without follow-up.

## Example

- Researcher: "You mentioned the tasking module. Before it existed, how did tasks get assigned?"
- Participant: "Phone calls, mostly. And a whiteboard."
- Researcher: "What did the whiteboard give you that the module doesn't?"
- Participant: "You could see the whole day at a glance."
- Insight: the module improved assignment but lost situational overview — a real, specific gap.

## Coaching note

Suggest the contrast question with the specific tool/process named, and include the rationale: baseline behaviour is the control condition; without it, reported benefits are unanchored.
