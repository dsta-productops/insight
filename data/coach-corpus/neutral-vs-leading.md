# Neutral vs. Leading Questions

## Principle

A leading question embeds the expected answer, an assumption, or an evaluation inside the question itself, biasing what the participant reports. Interview data is only as good as the questions are neutral. Participants exhibit acquiescence bias (agreeing with the interviewer) and social desirability bias (answering to look good), so even mild steering compounds.

## Common leading forms and neutral rewrites

| Leading | Why it biases | Neutral rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| "Don't you find the new layout easier?" | Presupposes 'easier'; invites agreement | "How does the new layout compare with the old one for you?" |
| "How much do you love the export feature?" | Presupposes positive affect | "What's your experience with the export feature?" |
| "Would you use this if it saved you time?" | Hypothetical plus embedded benefit | "Walk me through the last time you did this task." |
| "Was that frustrating?" | Supplies the emotion | "How did you feel when that happened?" |
| "So you basically want fewer clicks?" | Summarizing into the researcher's frame | "What would a better version of that look like for you?" |

## Additional leading mechanisms

- **Binary framing** ("Do you prefer A or B?") when the honest answer may be "neither" or "depends".
- **Loaded vocabulary** — importing the team's internal language ("the smart assistant") frames judgment before the participant speaks.
- **Question stacking** — asking two or three questions at once; participants answer the easiest one.
- **Premature summarizing** — paraphrasing the participant too early and too tightly, which they will politely accept.

## Detection patterns (when a coach should flag)

- Question begins with "Don't you", "Wouldn't you", "Isn't it".
- Question contains an evaluative adjective supplied by the researcher (easy, confusing, great, annoying) before the participant has used it.
- Question proposes a benefit or outcome inside a hypothetical ("if it saved you time").
- Yes/no framing where an open form would work.
- Multiple question marks' worth of questions in one utterance.

## Coaching note

When flagging, quote the biasing fragment, offer a neutral rewrite, and give the rationale: the participant's unprompted vocabulary and framing is the data; once the researcher supplies the frame, the answer measures compliance, not experience.
